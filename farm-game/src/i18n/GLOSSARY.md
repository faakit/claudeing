# Brazilian Portuguese glossary (pt-BR)

The words Tiny Acre uses in Portuguese, so every screen, line and letter says the same thing the same way. Add a
term here before using a new one in `pt-BR.json` or `data/pt-BR/*.json`. Choices marked **owner** are open: the
owner may prefer another word (the coordinator's provisional call keeps all four as written).

## Tone

- Warm, simple, everyday Brazilian Portuguese: the cozy tone of the English, never a literal copy of it.
- **Never gender the player.** The farmer can be anyone, so no "bem-vindo/bem-vinda", "cansado", "o novo fazendeiro",
  "obrigado" said to them. Use neutral turns: "Boas-vindas", "Sem energia!", "você sobe primeiro", "a pessoa".
- Short. The coach line is one row of 184 px and the goal plate 182 px; Portuguese runs 20-30% longer, so prefer
  the shorter of two good words ("lavrar" over "preparar a terra" where space is tight).
- No characters the pixel font lacks: `º`, `ª`, curly quotes, dashes (`—`), `€`. Use straight quotes and "lugar 2".
- Seasons are lower case inside a sentence ("no inverno"); the HUD and titles capitalise them ("Primavera 6").
- The 24-hour clock ("14:30", "às 2h"), and numbers with a dot for thousands ("1.234").

## Villager voices

| Who   | Voice in Portuguese                                                                                         |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rosa  | The old neighbour: gentle, unhurried, a little old-fashioned; short sentences about soil and Ilse. "A terra lembra." |
| Mara  | The shopkeeper: chatty and cheerful, exclamations, "Entra, entra!", calls the player "freguesia".          |
| Finn  | The fisher: laconic and calm, very short lines, a soft "né?", "viu?", "hein" instead of "friend".           |
| Orin  | The smith: terse, proverbs of the trade ("Ferramenta boa é meia colheita."), no wasted words.               |
| Clay  | The proud rival: colloquial and dry ("Tá bom", "pra mim", "Me vence"), competitive, softening into "parceria". |
| Ilse  | Her journal: practical notes in her own hand, short and sure ("Onde a água começa, a fazenda começa.").      |

## Names kept as they are

People: Rosa, Mara, Finn, Orin, Clay, Ilse, Senna, Tobin, the Holts. Places: **the Wend** (o Wend, masculine, like
"o rio"), **Wendhead**. In villagers' speech a name may take the article the way Brazilians talk ("a Ilse dizia"); UI
text uses the bare name.
