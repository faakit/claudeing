# Tiny Acre: The Dry Year (vision)

Read this first. It is the north star for every agent; [WORLD-PLAN.md](WORLD-PLAN.md) is the full design and
[MILESTONES.md](MILESTONES.md) the build order.

## The premise

The valley is called the Wend, after its river. Seven years ago a spring flood brought down the hillside at the
Narrows, and when the water went down the Wend had cut itself a new bed and left eastward, out of the valley. The
springs it fed went quiet. Orchards died standing. The mill stopped, the forge's bellows-wheel stopped, the
traders stopped coming, and family by family the valley emptied.

Five people stayed. **Rosa** kept her grandmother's last cherry tree alive with buckets. **Mara** kept the store
lit "so there's a light on when people come back". **Finn** fishes the woods lake because the *Heron*, the boat his
father built, sits on the mud where the river used to be. **Orin** mends hinges at a cold forge. **Clay** works his family's
fields alone, sells to the few buyers left, and does not believe anyone else will stay.

Rosa writes to you, the grandchild of Ilse, who kept the farm next to hers for forty years:

> You won't remember me. I farmed beside your grandmother Ilse.
> The river went east after the flood, and most folk went with it.
> Her fields are dust now, but the soil remembers. So do I.
> One more farmer, and the Wend might not be forgotten.
> The key is under the blue pot. Rosa

**What "dry" means (and what still has water).** The Dry Year is not a drought of weather. Rain still falls in
spring and fall; it just does not stay ("the valley drinks it by noon"). What is gone is the river and the springs
it fed. So:

- **Dry:** the farm's creek bed, the town river (a trickle two tiles wide down a wide bed of cracked mud), the Old
  Spring above the farm, Rosa's family orchard in the woods, the mill race, the hot spring. Grass on a broken map is straw-coloured.
- **Still wet:** the farm pond (a rain pond: the one thing the flood gave the farm), the woods lake (fed by deep
  springs, its shore lower than it was), rain itself. The watering can, the guided start's rain on day 2 and every
  fishing spot the game teaches early keep working exactly as now.
- **Fishing in year 1:** pond and lake as today; the town river holds only small fish until the Weir is rebuilt,
  then trout, salmon and its two legends swim back up.

## The emotional arc

The arc is told in **chapters of a life that keeps going**, never acts toward an ending.

- **Chapter one (year 1): a tired place notices you.** Day 1 is the guided start, unchanged in length, on a farm
  that is straw-coloured from fence to fence (owner's decision: the whole farm parched, palette only). By the end of the first week your fields are the only green in the valley, and one
  morning the whole farm turns from straw to green. Rosa hands you Ilse's seed tin and journal; its first
  page points uphill, at the Old Spring.
  Through summer, fall and winter it is one place at a time, one person at a time. Each broken place
  belongs to someone. Mending it is their wish come true and changes the map you walk on: the creek runs, the
  forge rings, the river comes home and lifts the *Heron*, dead trees bud. Each restoration teaches one new way to
  play. Families start writing that they are thinking of coming back, then they do: a lit window, a new face, a
  stall. Clay watches all of it from his fence, and slowly stops racing you and starts working beside you. Winter 28
  is a pause between chapters, not a conclusion: the year-end screen reads back what came back and says "See you in
  spring."
- **Chapter two (year 2): homecomings, and the valley's first full celebration.** Houses fill, Clay's board race runs
  on into the year until he calls you partner, and on fall 27 the valley holds its first full **Lantern Night** since the
  flood: each household brings a lantern made from its own mended place, you open the sluice at the weir, and the
  river carries them down the valley together, "for the ones who'd gone, so they'd know the way back". It is a
  milestone the valley reaches, not the end of the game: no credits, nothing that says "complete", and the next
  morning is an ordinary farm morning with something new in the mailbox.
- **Chapter three and on: the valley keeps growing.** Lantern Night every fall, with more lanterns as more households
  return (Mara's ledger still has names in it, and each can come home in a later content drop the same way); a notch
  on the river stone each spring; market days; the seasons dressing a living town; each mechanic's next step. The
  game is built to keep adding chapters.

**The player fantasy:** *"I brought this place back, and I can see it every time I walk through town."*

## Pillars

1. **The valley visibly heals.** Every restoration changes a map you walk across daily, not a menu. Something
   visible changes in the first week, then about every two to four weeks of play. If a feature cannot be seen in
   the world, it is not a restoration.
2. **Every mended place teaches a new verb.** Six broken places, six mechanics that play differently from each
   other and from what exists (channels, a rhythm forge, towing timber, a seed library, grafting, a market
   stall). Each ships small first. If only three places ship (the spring, the weir, the Seed House), the game is
   still whole.
3. **People, not bundles.** A place is asked for by the person who lost it, paid for with a little gold, a part you
   make, and a few days of help at the site that preview the new verb, and opened with that person. Losses are
   specific and fixable, and most are fixed by someone coming home.
4. **One thumb, five-minute sessions.** Every new verb is a tap, Action, walking or the existing paint gesture. A visit to a
   site is one tap a day. Nothing new needs two hands or a long sitting.
5. **Cozy, never punishing.** Nothing breaks again, nothing is lost by waiting, nobody leaves because you were
   slow. The valley waits for you; there is no deadline and no failure ending.

## What success feels like

- A first-time player understands why they are here before day 2 and feels the first change by the end of week one.
- A returning player can name the villager behind each mended place, and can point at what changed.
- The first full Lantern Night lands as a celebration the valley earned, not a finish line and not a score; the player
  wakes up the next morning wanting to see what the valley does next. Nothing in the game grades the valley: the yearly High Water notch
  records only how high the river came; the year two notches first stand side by side (usually year 3) is how you
  see how far the valley has come.
- A player who stops after year 1 has finished a chapter, not abandoned a story: four places mended, the first family
  home, Clay softening at the Winter Feast, and a year-end screen that reads back what they brought back.

## What the game is NOT

- **Not a story told in cutscenes.** One letter, short heart scenes (three lines, as today) and the world itself.
  No modal sequences longer than a heart event, and never on day 1.
- **Not a rescue from a villain.** No company, no curse, no magic. The flood was nobody's fault; the antagonist is
  absence.
- **Not bundles or a checklist building.** No "donate one of each" room. Restorations are infrastructure the
  valley lived by (water, iron, seed, trade), asked for by people.
- **Not a copy of a famous farm game.** No inherited farm from a dying grandparent's letter opened at a desk job,
  no community-centre bundles, no broken beach bridge, no spirits that fix things, no corporation, no yearly
  evaluation. See the originality notes in WORLD-PLAN.md.
- **Not a longer day 1.** The guided start (one line at a time, about 2-3 minutes to day 2) stays as tuned; the
  letter lives on the title screen.
- **Not a grind.** Restoration gold stays small next to the town projects; parts and work days are the cost.
- **Not a game that ends.** No final cutscene, no credits, no "you finished" screen. Milestones are celebrations inside
  a continuing life, and there is always a next season to see.

- Lantern Night (year 2, fall 27) is the valley's first full celebration, a milestone and not an ending; the valley
  keeps growing after it, and year 1 and year 2 read as chapters.
- Approved: the rhythm forge beside fishing; the *Heron* seen, not driven; towing behind the lab-map prototype gate;
